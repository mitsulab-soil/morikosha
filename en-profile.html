I graduated from the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Saitama University in March 2018, and from that April I spent eight years as a civil engineering technician at the Saitama Prefectural Government, supervising and maintaining construction of roads, rivers, slopes and parks, and handling disaster recovery.

The work ran from design review and cost estimation to site supervision after tendering and completion inspection. One project was the landslide recovery of a slope that collapsed in Typhoon Hagibis in 2019. I was the site engineer in charge, and the project was selected for the FY2024 Zenkensho (disaster category, urban division). After eight years on the commissioning side, I know first-hand how a project proceeds and where paperwork and money bring it to a halt.

I left the prefectural government in March 2026 and now work in the fields at an agricultural corporation. In construction and in farming alike, I heard the same words: "We can't find people," and "We do good work, but no one outside can see it." The substance of the work is sound, yet there is no way for outsiders to confirm it. I came to believe that what is missing is not skill but a form that gets the work across.

Why we started Morikosha

For eight years at the prefecture, I was on the side that commissioned and supervised construction. Many of the companies I met on site shared the same worry: young people do not come to work for them.

It is the same in farming, where I work now, and in landscaping. There are not enough people, and recruiting brings no one. I feel that reality up close.

I opened Morikosha to build an entry point that reaches people looking for work, products and services, with the aim of increasing joy in society.

How we work

  1. We publish only facts

    We build pages only from the information you give us and publicly available materials. Where an item cannot be filled in, we leave it out rather than invent it.

  2. We separate what is known from what is not

    We never write "always" or "guaranteed." When we give a figure, we put its source and date next to it.

  3. We write in the reader's words

    We avoid strings of jargon and write in the order of what to do. Your readers are people outside the industry.

We make no proposals that rely on discounts, referral fees or subsidies. The price is the same for everyone.
